Rebuilding freight procurement for EV cargo bound for Africa

Up to 30%Freight cost reduction
20+Empaneled agents
9Destination markets

The situation

I joined an electric-mobility manufacturer that ships electric motorcycles, lithium-ion battery modules and EV trucks from Asia to markets across East, West and Central Africa. Volumes were growing quickly, and freight was being bought shipment by shipment. Each quote was negotiated on its own, with no shared benchmark and no consistent way to compare agents.

What I did

  • Built a structured RFQ process across a network of 20+ empaneled forwarding agents, so every lane was quoted against the same scope and the same timeline.
  • Wrote standard operating procedures for ocean freight procurement, vendor evaluation and RFQ approval, so decisions could be traced and repeated.
  • Set up carrier and forwarder scorecards, and benchmarked rates lane by lane.
  • Took ownership of container demurrage and detention: daily tracking of free time, invoice validation and root-cause review of every charge.
  • Matched the cargo to the right mode. Containerised for bikes and battery modules. RORO or break-bulk for trucks.

The result

Freight costs came down by up to 30% on the lanes we retendered. Just as important, the business could see why a rate was what it was, and which agents were performing.

What I'd tell someone else

Lithium-ion cargo changes the conversation. Batteries ship as dangerous goods (Class 9), and not every agent, line or port handles them the same way. Put the DG questions in the RFQ itself. If they come up at booking, your cheapest quote can quietly turn into your most expensive one.

Running 20,000 TEU a year of scrap exports with five carriers

20,000TEU per year
5Carriers, direct
10%Cost reduction
20Team members

The situation

At Premier Info Assist I handled export logistics for a US recycling client shipping metal and plastic scrap, all in 40-foot containers, at around 20,000 TEU a year. At that volume, small differences in rate, free time or equipment availability add up to real money.

What I did

  • Negotiated freight rates directly with CMA CGM, Hapag-Lloyd, Maersk, MSC and COSCO.
  • Split volume across carriers so the client was never dependent on a single line for equipment or space.
  • Led a team of 20 handling bookings, documentation and shipment follow-up.

The result

We brought overall logistics cost down by 10% while keeping volume moving.

What I'd tell someone else

Volume gives you a seat at the table, but it doesn't negotiate for you. Carriers respond to a shipper who knows their own numbers: how many boxes, on which weeks, with what flexibility. Know your forecast better than the carrier does.

Moving 42 shipper-owned containers from Houston to Shanghai

42SOC containers
USHOU → CNSHATrade lane

The situation

Most shipments move in carrier-owned containers (COC). The line supplies the box and takes it back. This project moved 42 shipper-owned containers (SOC) from Houston to Shanghai, and it became one of the projects I'm proudest of.

Why SOC is different

With SOC, the shipper owns the equipment, so the shipper owns the problems the carrier normally absorbs:

  • The containers need to be in condition the carrier will accept for loading.
  • The booking has to be accepted specifically as SOC, and not every service or rate covers it.
  • Someone has to plan what happens to the boxes at destination.

What I'd tell someone else

Settle the equipment questions before the freight question. A good rate on an SOC booking means nothing if the boxes are rejected at the gate.

Running a forwarding branch next to Kandla and Mundra

15%Efficiency gain
2 yrsBranch leadership

The situation

I was Branch Manager at Fairmacs Shipping & Transport in Gandhidham, which serves Kandla and Mundra, two of India's busiest gateways on the west coast. The branch handled customer shipments end to end, and my job was the whole operation: the team, the customers and the branch's performance.

What I worked on

  • Day-to-day coordination with customers, carriers, CHAs and port-side partners.
  • Building routines the team could run without me, so one person's absence didn't stall a shipment.
  • Watching performance and fixing the steps that slowed shipments down.

The result

Operational efficiency at the branch improved by 15% over my time there.

What I'd tell someone else

Moving from managing shipments to managing a branch means your output is now other people's work. The most useful thing I did was make the routine predictable, so my time went to the exceptions.

Dispatching home textiles to US big-box retailers

25%Faster delivery
4Major US retailers

The situation

At Welspun India in Anjar I handled export dispatch for US retail customers including Walmart, Kohl's, Kmart and JC Penney. Big-box retailers issue strict routing and compliance requirements, and a miss can mean chargebacks or delayed acceptance at the distribution centre.

What I worked on

  • Planning dispatches against each retailer's requirements and timelines.
  • Coordinating between production, packing and the outbound logistics team so cargo left on time.

The result

Delivery time came down by 25%.

What I'd tell someone else

For retail exports, the customer's rulebook is the plan. Read the routing guide before production finishes, not after the cargo is packed.
